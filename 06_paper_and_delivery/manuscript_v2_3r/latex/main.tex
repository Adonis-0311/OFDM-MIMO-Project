\begin{abstract}
Sparse tensor recovery is a natural model for mmWave MIMO-OFDM integrated sensing and communication, but dictionary quantization limits physically interpretable parameter estimates. We study learned, bounded interpolation between Tensor-OMP grid supports and deterministic local-refinement candidates. The local three-dimensional implementation learns one scalar and improves measurement-domain NMSE over grid Tensor-OMP by 5.8956 dB on average across $L\in\{2,4,8\}$ in a five-seed, 450-scene synthetic study (seed-mean 95\% CI $[5.7830,6.0083]$ dB). A separate 210-parameter feature controller for two-dimensional CDL measurements predicts delay and angle interpolation scales and is trained with permutation-invariant, alias-aware physical supervision. On frozen held-out CDL-A/C evaluation it yields gains of 6.2286 dB in channel NMSE, 10.1241 ns in delay RMSE, and $0.5277^\circ$ in projected-angle RMSE. Zero-shot CDL-D degrades channel and delay estimates, bounding the profile-generalization claim. A matched dense-tensor comparison further shows that the NOMP-inspired path is the accuracy-oriented point whereas the learned scalar is a lower-compute amortized alternative. A separate deterministic repeated-refinement diagnostic tests a restricted high-load computation--quality trade-off but does not support an SNR-robust early-stop claim. These results support a compute--accuracy and external-robustness view of bounded post-support refinement, not accuracy supremacy or a fully integrated deep-unfolded network.
\end{abstract}
