\section{Limitations and Future Work}

The reported results combine controlled synthetic/off-grid evidence with standards-aligned held-out CDL-A/C evaluation. They do not constitute instrumented-receiver or profile-universal validation of an ISAC receiver. The one-parameter local model, the 210-parameter CDL controller, and the deterministic A4 depth diagnostic are separate implementations; a unified multi-stage learned estimator remains unvalidated.

The first limitation is external validity across channel families. The trained controller improves aggregate channel, delay, and projected-angle metrics on held-out CDL-A/C seeds, but zero-shot CDL-D degrades channel NMSE and delay RMSE. The result is therefore profile-scoped. DeepMIMO remains limited to scalar-transfer proxies without a matched trained physical evaluator, and hardware-in-the-loop evidence is absent.

The second limitation is the scope of adaptive depth. The positive early-stop evidence is restricted to high-load settings at the core 20 dB SNR condition, with the strongest main-text support for $L\geq32$. The lower-load $L=16$ cell is a boundary case. The SNR sweep at $L=32$ is also mixed: the minimum SNR-cell mean saving is 21.8750\%, and the maximum NMSE gap reaches 0.6834 dB. A threshold-frontier diagnostic reinforces this boundary: finer scalar-threshold selection does not recover a stable SNR-axis policy, and the diagnostic test-oracle frontier still has a minimum SNR mean saving of 0.0000\%. A subsequent SNR/curve-feature gate diagnostic also fails to rescue the claim: the lightweight kNN gate has a minimum SNR mean saving of 21.8750\% and a maximum SNR gap of 0.6558 dB, while the per-curve quality oracle still falls to 21.8750\% minimum SNR mean saving. Thus, the plateau gate is best interpreted as a useful high-load efficiency mechanism under the tested core condition, not as a depth policy that is stable across SNR.

The third limitation is robustness to hardware impairments. The combined impairment stress test does not yet support a main robustness claim. It is still useful as failure-mode evidence, because it shows where the current training and refinement recipe is brittle, but a stronger contribution would require robust training or explicit impairment-aware calibration.

The fourth limitation is comparator scope. G2 now uses 30 held-out scenes per target count and seed, and the matched campaign uses 20 paired scenes in every SNR--target-count--seed cell. The NOMP-inspired comparator nevertheless assumes known target order, a dense tensor, and the matched sinusoidal generator; it is not the sparse-resource CFAR formulation of NOMP-OFDM-ISAC. Separable forward-backward ESPRIT remains timing-only because the current implementation does not pair components across axes. The resulting Pareto plot therefore establishes a matched-regime compute--accuracy trade-off, not universal dominance.

The fifth limitation is theoretical coverage. The CRLB analysis checks analytic derivative consistency and a single-target high-SNR trend. It does not establish tightness for the full multi-target asymptotic regime, nor does it remove the support-conditioning assumption behind the local off-grid argument.

Future work should therefore integrate the learned paths into one executable estimator, add matched-accuracy continuous-recovery comparators, enlarge the local statistical study, close the CDL-D and DeepMIMO profile-shift gap, and train against hardware impairments.
